Everything here
has been on fire.

Thirty-four seats around an open hearth in the Arts District. One set menu, cooked over oak and vine cuttings, at 6 and at 8:45.

There is no stove.

There is a hearth, a wood pile, and someone who has been watching it since two in the afternoon.

Four hours
before you sit

  1. 2:00

    The oak goes in

    Split oak and old vine cuttings from a grower in Paso Robles, lit once and not again until Sunday closes.

  2. 4:30

    It stops being flame

    Nothing is cooked over fire. It is cooked over what fire leaves, which is the entire difference and takes two hours to arrive.

  3. 5:15

    The bed is raked

    Coals moved by hand into three zones, hot to barely warm, so a whole fish and a leek can share one hearth.

  4. 6:00

    You sit down

    Close enough to feel it on the side of your face. That is not an accident of the layout.

One menu

It changes when the market changes, which is most weeks. This is what is on it tonight. $115, seven courses, and the kitchen will cook around an allergy but not around a preference.

  1. i

    Leeks, buried

    Put directly into the ash for forty minutes, skins peeled back at the pass, hazelnut and old sherry vinegar.

  2. ii

    Bread and the good butter

    Sourdough on the grill bars until it blisters. Cultured butter, smoked salt. You get more if you ask.

  3. iii

    Bone marrow

    The one thing that never comes off. Roasted in the mouth of the hearth, parsley, caper, a spoon.

  4. iv

    Sea bream, whole

    Over vine cuttings in a hinged basket, turned twice. Served on the bone because that is where it is best.

  5. v

    Short rib, 45 days

    Dry-aged in the back, cut thick, laid on oak coals for eleven minutes and rested for eleven more.

  6. vi

    Beets in the embers

    Cooked whole overnight in the dying bed, then cold, with buttermilk and black pepper.

  7. vii

    Burnt honey ice cream

    Honey taken past the point most people stop. Slightly bitter, on purpose.

Thirty-four
stools

No tables. One counter that wraps the hearth on three sides, so every seat is the good seat and nobody is sat by the bathroom.

It is loud, it is warm, and it smells like the fire for the whole two hours. If that sounds like the wrong evening, we would honestly rather you knew now.
