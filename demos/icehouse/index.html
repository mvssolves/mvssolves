Union Ice & Cold Storage,
1924–1987

Built to hold produce coming off the rail before it went to the market four blocks west. Six floors of cork-lined rooms held a little above freezing by a plant in the basement that ran continuously for sixty-three years.

It has been empty since 1987, which is the only reason the steel, the brick and the windows are all still original. Nobody was here to improve them.

  1. 1924 Built for Union Ice, six floors, one elevator
  2. 1941 Requisitioned; held rations for the Port of LA
  3. 1987 Plant shut down, building sealed
  4. 2024 Bought, surveyed, structure found sound
  5. 2026 Twenty-eight lofts, first completions in the autumn

Specification

Kept

Steel columns and beams, exposed and fireproofed to code. Brick as found, repointed only where it was failing. Original crittall windows, restored and double-glazed in the existing frames.

Added

Radiant slab heating, a new stair and lift core in the old chute shaft, and a roof deck around the water tank. Nothing added touches the street elevation.

Not added

No gym, no lounge, no co-working room, no concierge. They would raise the monthly charge by about $340 and be empty by year two.

Monthly

$0.41 per square foot, covering structure, insurance, the lift and the roof. Published in full before you sign, not after.
