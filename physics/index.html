Study 04 — spring field

Five thousand springs want a cursor.

Every dot is a damped spring anchored to a pixel of rendered text, scattered by an inverse-square force from your pointer. Without a pointer there is nothing to scatter it.

Below is how it is built.

Engineering note

Thousands of bodies on the main thread

The constraint

Around 5,000 independently simulated particles at 60fps, in JavaScript, on the main thread, while the rest of the page stays responsive. That is a 16ms budget shared with layout and paint.

Why the obvious route fails

An array of particle objects allocates on every frame and hands the garbage collector thousands of short-lived references. The result is not a low frame rate — it is periodic stalls, which read worse than consistent slowness.

What I did instead

Every quantity lives in a flat Float32Array — positions, velocities, targets, per-particle seed. No objects, no allocation inside the loop, nothing for the collector to sweep. All particles are drawn as one batched path with a single fill, then a second pass tints only those moving fast enough to matter.

The trade-off I accepted

Particles do not collide with each other. Pairwise collision is O(n²), and at this count it would need spatial hashing that buys nothing visually — the anchors already keep the field readable. Repulsion comes from a single point: your cursor.
