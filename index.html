01

The maths, not the library call

If an off-the-shelf plugin can do it, you do not need me — you need twenty minutes and the docs. I get hired for the brief that has no plugin: the shader, the simulation, the scroll system that has to feel physically attached to the page.

Evidence — the hero above ships no 3D library at all.

02

It degrades or it does not ship

Effects that only work on the machine they were built on are a liability you inherit at launch. Every piece here has a defined failure path: no WebGL2, no GPU headroom, or reduced-motion enabled, and the layout still reads perfectly.

Evidence — kill WebGL and every word on this site survives.

03

Measured, never hoped for

"It runs fine on my laptop" is not a performance strategy. These pages measure their own frame time and move the render resolution to hold the budget on hardware neither of us has tested.

Evidence — press P for live frame rate and render scale.

The motion and WebGL layer of the front-end, plus the marketing-site build around it. I take the brief a studio can't staff internally — the shader hero, the scroll sequence, the thing the pitch deck promised.

Page builders, template reskins, and anything where "make it pop" is the whole brief. I'm also not the right person for backend-heavy platform work.
